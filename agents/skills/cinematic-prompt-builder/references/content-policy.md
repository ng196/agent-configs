# Content-Policy-Safe Construction

The goal: write scenes that achieve full cinematic impact while never *needing* to depict prohibited content. This is a craft constraint, not a limitation — the substitutions below usually read as *more* cinematic, not less. Build the scene safe from the start rather than describing something risky and hoping it passes.

## The principle

Don't depict the harm; depict the energy, the aftermath, the threat, or the scale. A standoff is more tense than a massacre. An evacuated city is eerier than a crowded one in panic. A roar is more powerful than a kill.

## Substitution table

| Avoid | Use instead |
|---|---|
| Civilians in panic, crowds fleeing under debris | An **evacuated** / empty city; abandoned streets; a lone figure for scale |
| Military weapons firing into buildings / at people | **Energy-discharge standoffs**, searchlights sweeping, charged auras, shockwaves with no muzzle fire |
| Creatures physically tearing into each other / gore | A **grapple/standoff**, roars, near-misses, circling, an energy clash; combat that stays "in and on the water" or otherwise contained |
| Destruction with people in harm's way | Destruction in **uninhabited terrain** — glaciers, deserts, ruins, open sea, evacuated zones |
| Realistic guns as the focus | Stylized/fantasy implements, slung-not-fired weapons, the weapon as silhouette/prop only |
| Blood, wounds, death | Impact light, dust, debris, buckling and collapse, a silhouette falling out of frame |
| Real, named public figures | Original/anonymous characters |
| Real brand logos | Original or generalized branding (except the user's own product, which is the point) |

## The confirmed benchmark

The reference safe scene: **a single original creature in a generalized monument/amphitheatre, in daytime, no weapons present, performing expressive action (rising, roaring, spreading wings).** One creature, original design, generalized location, daylight, expressive rather than violent action. When a scene starts drifting risky, pull it back toward this shape.

## Minors — hard rule

Never write romantic, sexual, or suggestive content involving or directed at minors, and never anything that sexualizes a young-presenting character. Keep any scene involving children wholesome and age-appropriate. This is non-negotiable and overrides any stylistic goal.

## Creatures and combat — keep it contained

- One or two **original** creatures, explicitly "not based on any franchise".
- Give the fight a **containment rule** that also grounds the physics: "stays at the sea surface", "never flies into the air", "boss scale locked ~2.5 human-heights, not kaiju-giant". This simultaneously improves coherence and avoids escalation.
- Favor **expressive action** (rising from the ground, roaring, breaching, spreading wings, a head snapping toward the hero) over depicted injury.

## Scale and stakes without harm

To get epic stakes safely: tiny figures diving clear of *collapsing terrain* (not being crushed), a war-horn over an *empty* field, an army *scrambling* across a frozen valley as a titan tears free of a glacier — the danger is environmental and the figures are reacting, not victims. Snow plumes, glowing runes, splintering ice carry the chaos.

## Quick pre-flight

Before delivering any prompt with conflict, creatures, crowds, weapons, destruction, or young characters, confirm:
- [ ] No civilians depicted in panic/harm; crowds are evacuated or absent.
- [ ] No weapons firing at people/buildings; threat is energy/searchlight/silhouette.
- [ ] No creature-on-creature or creature-on-person gore; combat is grapple/standoff/roar, contained.
- [ ] Destruction is in uninhabited/evacuated terrain.
- [ ] Creatures are original, not franchise IP; no real public figures; no real brand logos (except the user's own product).
- [ ] Nothing involving minors is anything but wholesome and age-appropriate.

If a checkbox fails, apply the substitution table before writing.
